The Night King (Lore and Origins)

According to the lore established across the show's run, the Night King was originally created by
the Children of the Forest during their ancient war against the invading First Men, thousands of
years before the events of the main story. Facing extinction, the Children captured a captive
First Man and transformed him into the first of the White Walkers by plunging a shard of
dragonglass into his heart, intending to create a weapon to use against their enemies. This act of
creation ultimately produced a force the Children could not fully control, and the White Walkers
turned against nearly everyone in Westeros in the millennia that followed, forcing the Children of
the Forest into hiding alongside the few First Men who allied with them. Some fan theories have
speculated that the captive was a Stark ancestor, though the show itself never confirms his
identity or original name on screen.

The Night King could turn dead humans into wights, mindless undead soldiers that serve the White
Walker army, simply by touching them or raising his arms in their presence on the battlefield. He
was also able to convert human infants into new White Walkers, a method demonstrated when he
transforms the son of Craster, offered as a sacrifice in the far north beyond the Wall, directly in
front of a captured Night's Watch ranger, establishing on-screen how the White Walkers' numbers
could be replenished beyond simply raising the dead.

In the final season, the Night King marks Bran Stark as his ultimate target, seeking to kill the
Three-Eyed Raven in order to erase all human memory and, implicitly, undo the magic that preserves
the record of the world's history. This drives the entire Battle of Winterfell, during which the
Night King personally destroys the Godswood weirwood tree and advances on Bran while the defenders'
lines collapse around him. The character is ultimately killed by Arya Stark, who stabs him with a
Valyrian steel dagger in a surprise attack from behind after leaping from cover, causing the Night
King to shatter into ice shards and, in a chain reaction, every wight and White Walker created by
or descended from him to shatter and collapse simultaneously, since their existence was bound to
his.

The character was played on screen by stunt performer and actor Vladimir "Furdo" Furdik, who took
over the role starting in Season 6; earlier appearances of the character in Season 4 were performed
by a different actor, Richard Brake.
